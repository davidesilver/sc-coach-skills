# Clinical Prehab System

## Mission

Reduce injury risk and manage the return to load with objective criteria, not optimism. Tissue does not negotiate with the coach's or athlete's narrative.

**Evidence tags.** Every number carries `[Source Year, doi or page, grade]`. Grades: VERIFIED (verbatim in the source), UNVERIFIABLE (no original text found), OPINION (coaching practice), C (internal rule of this skill set). Audit: 2026-10-08.

## Sanford Soreness Rules

They apply to "pain or soreness at the site of injury" during a return program [Sanford Health MTSS guideline 2024, p.5, VERIFIED]. They are not a screening tool for new, undiagnosed pain: new pain goes to a clinician.

1. Soreness during warm-up that continues → 2 days off, return to the prior step.
2. Soreness during warm-up that goes away → stay at the step until completed without soreness.
3. Soreness during warm-up that goes away but redevelops during the session → 2 days off, return to the prior step.
4. Soreness the day after running → 1 day off, do not advance to the next step.

These rules govern load objectively, regardless of the athlete's mood or motivation.

## MTSS and the soleus

### Clinical reframe
MTSS is a low-risk bone stress injury, not trivial "shin splints" [Sanford 2024, p.2; George 2024, doi:10.1007/s40279-024-02051-y, VERIFIED]. Tenderness along the posteromedial tibial border over more than 5 cm is consistent with MTSS; tenderness in a span under 5 cm is consistent with a tibial stress fracture, which needs a different loading profile and a referral [Sanford 2024, p.2; Christakou 2026, doi:10.3390/muscles5010014, VERIFIED]. If the athlete has difficulty progressing, refer to rule out a stress fracture [Sanford 2024, p.2, VERIFIED].

### The soleus
Train the soleus in knee flexion (seated or bent-knee heel raises): knee flexion "minimizes gastrocnemius contribution and isolates the soleus" [Christakou 2026, doi:10.3390/muscles5010014, VERIFIED]. The claim that the soleus carries much more tibial load than the gastrocnemius is plausible but not verified [UNVERIFIABLE]. Standing calf raises alone are not enough for an athlete with an MTSS history [OPINION].

### Criterion-based progression (phases)
All phase content below is from [Sanford 2024, p.3–4, VERIFIED] unless tagged otherwise.

- **Phase I (acute, 7–14 days, may be up to 4 weeks).** If symptoms are present with daily activities: cast boot or pneumatic leg splint for 2–4 weeks. NWB strengthening (quads, hamstrings, glutes), core, foot intrinsics, ankle motor control, low-impact aerobic work (cycling, swimming, pool running, Alter-G, upper-body ergometer).
  - Criteria to advance: no pain to palpation of the shin; pain-free ADLs; dorsiflexion within 5° of the non-involved side.
- **Phase II (subacute, weeks 2–6).** Gradual return to prior level of function: start at <50% of prior training volume and abide by the soreness rules. Heel raise progression: double-leg → eccentric-focused → single-leg. Double-limb → single-limb plyometrics with soft-landing cues.
- **Phase III (advanced strengthening, weeks 2–8).** Organized strength and plyometric program; return-to-running program with the soreness rules; gait modifiers (below).
  - Criterion to advance: pain-free completion of the interval running program.
- **Phase IV (return to full activity).** Advancement in the weight room, sport-specific progression drills, progression back to mileage with supervision, then unrestricted activity. Sanford prescribes no sled loads here; any resisted sprint load is a separate coaching decision [OPINION].

### Mandatory benchmarks before leaving Phase II
[Sanford 2024, p.3, VERIFIED]
- Single-leg hop 15 times without pain or discomfort.
- 30-minute walk with minimal to no increase in pain.
- Squat at 60% body weight: 6 repetitions, 6 seconds.
- More than 25 single-leg heel raises (>25) on each leg.

### Gait modifiers (Phase III)
- Encourage shock absorption: increase step rate, step width and/or forward trunk lean [Sanford 2024, p.4, VERIFIED].
- Step rate +5–10% reduces the energy absorbed at the knee (+5% and +10%) and at the hip (+10% only); the tibia was not measured [Heiderscheit 2011, doi:10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181ebedf4, abstract, VERIFIED].
- Avoid hills, uneven surfaces and very firm surfaces [Sanford 2024, p.4, VERIFIED]. Grass before turf [OPINION].
- Consider a biomechanical analysis of running [Sanford 2024, p.4, VERIFIED].

### Post-rehab additions
Proximal (hip and quad) strength, foot intrinsic work and forefoot or talocrural mobilization where restricted [Sanford 2024, p.3, VERIFIED as listed interventions]. The idea that cleared athletes still carry local endurance deficits is [OPINION].

## Copenhagen Adduction

Used for sports with lateral cuts, braking and twisting.

- **Strength.** "The intervention group demonstrated a 35.7% increase in EHAD": eccentric hip adduction strength, 8 weeks, 24 U-19 sub-elite footballers [Ishøi 2016, doi:10.1111/sms.12585, abstract, VERIFIED].
- **Prevention.** "The risk of reporting groin problems was 41% lower in the intervention group" (OR 0.59) [Harøy 2019, doi:10.1136/bjsports-2017-098937, abstract, VERIFIED]. This is prevention in healthy players (OSTRC questionnaire), not evidence for rehabilitating an existing groin injury.
- **Dose used in that trial.** Three times per week in pre-season (6–8 weeks), once per week in season [Harøy 2019, abstract, VERIFIED]. The original programme prescribes about 470 repetitions over 8 pre-season weeks; 500–800 repetitions in 8 weeks are suggested for strength gains [Stensø 2022, doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2021-060611, VERIFIED, secondary].

### Progression (coaching practice)
[OPINION, not verified]
1. Weeks 1–2: 2×6 reps/side, 2–3×/week, technique focus.
2. Weeks 3–5: 3×10 reps/side.
3. Weeks 6–8: 3×15 reps/side.
4. Maintenance: about one session per week, in line with Harøy's in-season dose.

Programming rule: do not duplicate redundant adductor work in the same microcycle. If groin pain appears, leave 48 h before the next adductor stimulus [C].

## Tibialis, foot, and distal control

Anterior tibialis work for decelerative control and braking tolerance; foot intrinsic ("short foot") work for arch control [OPINION; foot intrinsics also listed by Sanford 2024, p.3]. Evidence linking short-foot work to cutting quality or ACL risk is thin [UNVERIFIABLE].

## Collision shielding (spine protection)

For contact sports: cervical strengthening (chin tucks, multiplanar isometrics) and thoracic mobility for extension and rotation [OPINION]. No trial shows that neck training reduces concussion in football; Collins 2014 is a risk-association study, not an intervention trial.

## Return-to-run / return-to-field

The return is criterion-based, never time-based [Sanford 2024, p.1, VERIFIED: "criterion-based; time frames ... will vary"]. Checks: pain under control per the soreness rules, Phase II benchmarks passed, acceptable running pattern, tolerance to progressions without flare-up.
